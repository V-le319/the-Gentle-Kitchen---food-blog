title: What My Grandmother Never Wrote Down
date: 12 September 2026

heading: She never measured. She just knew.

My grandmother never used measuring cups. She used her palm, her eyes, the sound a pan makes when it is ready. When I asked her once how much fish sauce to add, she looked at me the way you look at someone asking a question the room has already answered. "Until it tastes right," she said, and went back to stirring.

For years I found this frustrating. I wanted numbers. I wanted a recipe I could hand to someone else exactly as it was given to me. But she had no interest in writing anything down, and I think now that this wasn't carelessness. It was a kind of faith — a belief that the knowledge lived in the doing, not in the paper, and that anyone who truly wanted to learn would have to stand beside her long enough to absorb it.

I didn't stand beside her long enough. That is the quiet grief underneath this essay. There are dishes I remember the taste of but not the shape of, gone now the way a voice fades from memory before the face does. I have tried to recreate them from instinct alone, the same way she cooked, and I fail most of the time in small, forgivable ways.

But something has stayed with me anyway. Not the recipes exactly, but the posture she brought to the stove — unhurried, attentive, willing to adjust based on what was in front of her rather than what a page told her to expect. She taught me, without ever saying so, that cooking is a form of paying attention. That a recipe is only a rough map, and the real navigation happens by taste, by smell, by the small adjustments only a person standing there can make.

So I have started writing things down, not because I disagree with her, but because I know I am not her, and my palm has not yet learned what hers knew. Someday, maybe, I won't need the page either. Until then, I write down what I can, and I leave space in the margins for the things that can only be learned by standing at the stove, the way she once stood, quietly, until it tasted right.
