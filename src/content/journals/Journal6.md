There is a version of starting over that looks dramatic: the clean slate, the bold declaration, the sudden reinvention. That has never been my version. My version looks more like a pot of rice cooking quietly on a stove I am still learning to trust, in a kitchen that isn't fully unpacked, on a day that otherwise looks unremarkable.

I have started over more than once, and each time I expect it to feel like a beginning. Instead it feels like continuation with the volume turned down. The same hands that once knew a different kitchen relearning the sound of a new stove, the same instincts adjusting to a different water, a different heat, a different rhythm of light coming through a different window in the afternoon.

Cooking, more than almost anything else, has taught me that starting over doesn't require starting from nothing. I bring my grandmother's instinct for taste, whatever I learned from cooking for one, the quiet lessons collected over onions and broths and years of small kitchen failures. None of that disappears just because the room around it changed. Starting over slowly means carrying forward what still serves you, and only slowly, gently, letting go of what doesn't.

I think this is why I trust the slow version more than the dramatic one. A dramatic reinvention asks you to discard everything and begin as someone new. A slow one asks something gentler: keep cooking, keep tasting, keep adjusting the salt until it's right, and let the new version of your life take shape the way a dish does, one considered addition at a time.

Some evenings I stand at this new stove and it still feels unfamiliar, my elbow bumping a cabinet that used to be somewhere else. But the rice still turns out fine. The broth still finds its flavor eventually. And I've started to believe that this is what starting over actually looks like, not a clean break, but a familiar hand slowly learning a new kitchen, patient enough to trust that it will feel like home again.
